# Recommendations, taste, home layout

- **EngineCard's `MatchedTags` must be safe to display.** Both semantic and fallback recommendation hydration remove tags marked `is_spoiler` in the candidate's own `tags_v2` before taking four labels. The embedding vocabulary's global spoiler flag is insufficient: a tag can be a spoiler for one series and ordinary for another. Keep spoiler filtering in hydration so it does not change ranking, and use the filtered list for card chips.

- **Discover's side-interest rows use minority core story tags from the visible shelf** (`SideInterestRailService`, `GET recommendations/discover/side-interests`). A tag qualifies only when at least two distinct works mark it core in the embedding index, it is a non-spoiler `TagMath.IsStoryCategory` tag (no character traits), and all its occurrences stay under the 35% library ceiling; no genre or unweighted-tag fallback. Fully incognito, below-neutral rated and above-ceiling series, and anything outside the seed snapshot's `Effective.EligibleIds`, are excluded before selecting. At most two rows of up to 20 picks, with a vector-index filter recheck because the SQL fallback cannot filter tags. The rows have no expanded view, since querying only their SeedIds would lose the theme constraint. No index or fewer than three matching picks means no row.

- **The behavioural channel is a second vector space, not a third graph** (`Maki.Metadata.Taste`, `{ConfigDir}/taste-vectors.db`, kill switch `recommendations.tastevectors`). Item vectors factorized from AniList reading lists, scored as a cosine beside the text one in the same row pass. It reaches ~95k of the ~126k indexed rows against the co-read graph's ~41k, which is the point: a pair table is a lookup and is empty for most of the catalogue. `MangaBakaRecommendation.TasteMatch` is its own "why" and must not be folded into `CoRead` — they answer different questions off the same file, and switching co-read off still costs measurable relevance.
- **Installing the taste artifact invalidates the whole vector index** (`VectorIndexCache` loads it per row) rather than swapping a file like the graph caches, so `TasteVectorJob` runs last of the staggered artifact downloads and must not run while the first index build is in progress.
- **`Maki.Core.Recommendations.TasteTuning` and `Maki.Metadata.Taste.TasteVectorTuning` are unrelated.** The first is seed *weighting* (how much each of a reader's series steers the profile); the second is the behavioural *channel*. A file using both needs one of them qualified.
- **Anything measured only against the crowd pair graphs is suspect in two specific directions** (details and numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md`): they over-represent famous titles, and they under-represent *affinity* — same creator, same franchise. A change that suppresses an affinity relationship looks free there and costs real relevance against held-out reading lists. Both biases surface in the eval's `pop` column before they surface anywhere else.
- **Catalogue filters are rules** (`CatalogueRule`/`CatalogueTerm` in `Maki.Core.Recommendations`, `RecommendationFilters.Rules`). A rule is `all`/`any`/`none` over genres and tags, rules AND together, and the legacy `Genres`/`Tags` lists still apply as an implicit `all`. `VectorIndex.Plan`/`Matches` is the exact path: tags, `Subtags` (via `TagSubtrees`, built from `name_path` because the dump only sometimes carries implied parents) and `Central` (weight class `Defining` or above). The other two evaluators are name-only: `BuildClause` (the dump SQL path) reduces each rule to its genres in the direction that **widens** (an `any` rule holding a tag is dropped whole), and `RecommendationFilters.MatchesNames` matches plain name lists (`DiscoverService`, `LibraryRailFilter`, `FollowedCreatorReleaseService`). Anything with a tag term must route through the index (`RecommendationFilters.NeedsTags`). An unknown name sinks an `all` rule and drops out of `any`/`none`. **`Hidden` is the per-user never-show list** (`discover.hidden`, `HiddenContentService`), always set by the controller, never by the request (`Sanitize` strips it). It is separate from `Rules` because exact-title search hits bypass the rules in `SemanticSearcher.RankCandidates` but must not bypass this. Instance-wide caches apply it as a post-filter with `RefillRailSize` headroom: the shared rails, relations in `POST recommendations` (which skip catalogue filters on purpose), and the series page's "More like this" and "Related" rails (filtered in `SeriesController`). Taste insights is cached per user, so the list goes into each group's scan plan and cache key instead. Discover presets live in `SavedFilters` with `Scope = discover` and a `SearchDefaultsSpec`; the Library controller filters to `Scope = library`, so a preset never crosses between the two shapes. On the client, `useTermFilters` holds a simple view and a rules view over one filter; `toSimple` decides whether a rule set fits the simple view.

- **Search also returns series the vector index does not hold** (`UnindexedHits`, `SemanticSearcher.UnindexedCandidatesAsync`): unrated series and anything newer than the embeddings, found by exact or near title, the lexical channel or the credit channel, at most 10 per free-text answer (`MaxUnindexed`). A creator-only query (`author:"Name"` with no text) skips that cap on purpose: it is a creator's work list ranked by popularity, so every visible unindexed work merges in and only `limit` bounds the answer. The dump checks them for state, type, content rating, creator scope and `Hidden`, the last on their own `tags_v2` through `VectorIndex.IsHidden` so subtag and central terms mean what they mean on a row. Preference filters cannot be tested there, so while one is set only an exact title is offered. Exact and near titles keep their title-distance tier; the rest compete on their lexical and credit channel scores plus the popularity prior, so a descriptive query is not led by obscure title matches.
- **The creator filter is names on the wire and ids in the scan** (`RecommendationFilters.Credits` / `CreditIds`, `CreditFilter`, `SearchDefaultsSpec.Credits`). `Credits` is stored by name because `CreditIndex` renumbers on every rebuild; `HiddenContentService.ApplyAsync` resolves it into `CreditIds` (server-set, stripped by `Sanitize`), so anything that skips `ApplyAsync`/`ScopeAsync` silently ignores creators. Every credit **unions** whatever its role, unlike the search box's `author:x studio:y`, which intersects. Callers adding their own credit restriction (the creator page, `author:` search) must `RestrictTo` the `CreditMask` rather than overwrite it. Library rails match the series' own comma-joined credit strings instead (`LibraryRailFilter.MatchesCredits`), trying each part and each adjacent pair since the join cannot tell "Ito, Junji" from two people. The follow list is `discover.following` (`FollowedCreatorsSpec`); its `following` rail is a client-side catalogue feed of those credits, `newest`, owned excluded ("New" is `published_start_date`).
- **Follow notifications use MangaBaka's id order as the added-on date** (`FollowedCreatorReleaseService`, `FollowedCreatorReleaseJob`, inbox type `FollowedCreatorRelease`). MangaBaka numbers series as they are added, so one instance watermark (`discover.following.watermark`, set to `CreditIndex.MaxSeriesId`) splits old from new with no per-follow state. The first run seeds it silently. A series whose credits arrive in a later dump than the series itself is missed. The link is `/creator/{name}?open={id}`, which opens the detail card directly, because a new entry is usually unrated and so not in the vector index the creator grid lists from.

- **Custom rails are a saved filter plus a source, and each source reuses the surface it imitates** (`CustomRailSpec`, `CustomRailService`, `api/v1/rails`). They live in `SavedFilters` with the placement as the scope (`homerail`/`discoverrail`), which keeps them out of both preset controllers. `recommendations` is `RecommendationService.GetAsync` taking `Similar` only; `catalogue` is `GetFeedAsync` with owned and suppressed ids folded into the scan as `FilterPlan.Exclude`, never post-filtered, so a rail is not cut short; `library` filters `db.Series`, through the vector index only when a tag, minimum score or the popularity sort needs it and `LibraryRailFilter.MatchesLocal` otherwise. Rail pools count against their own `PoolOrigin.Rail` budget in `RecommendationPoolCache`, so rails never evict the Recommended tab's pool; the scan lock is still global, which is why the client fetches a rail only as it nears the viewport. Rails are `rail:{id}` keys in each page's layout, merged by `PageLayouts.Merge`. Library rails are Home-only.
- **Discover's "Based on your recent activity" rail is the one per-user rail on that page** (`RecentActivityRailService`, `GET recommendations/discover/recent`), fetched apart from `GET discover`, whose rails are cached instance-wide. It only narrows `RecommendationService`'s seeds: the 6 most recently read series by `ReadCounts.ReadFor`, `Incognito.Full` excluded by hand, and each seed must be in the snapshot's `Effective.EligibleIds` (`RecommendationService` only checks an explicit seed against the observed population, so skipping this steers by ignored, thumbed-down and low-rated titles).
- **Franchise crowding is fixed by re-ordering, never by dropping picks.** `RecommenderTuning.MaxPerFranchise` (the drop) ships off because readers who finish something go on to read the rest, so dropping costs nDCG. `RecommendationService` stamps each pick with its same-work component (`SemanticRecommender.FranchisesAsync`, the vector index's `FranchiseGraph` column) and runs `Spread`, which keeps everything and each franchise's internal order and only guarantees `FranchiseSpacing` (8) between two members of one component. Relations need a real cap: `RecentActivityRailService.MaxRelatedPerFranchise` limits how many of the six leading slots one franchise takes, keyed on the component because two seeds can share a franchise. The eval harness calls `SemanticRecommender` directly and never sees any of this.
- **"Hide this and its franchise"** (`RecommendationFeedbackService.HideFranchiseAsync`, `POST recommendations/feedback/{id}/franchise`) uses `SemanticRecommender.FranchiseMembersAsync`, the same component the rails space out, and falls back to `MangaBakaLocalStore.GetSameWorkIdsAsync` (relation types via `FranchiseGraph.SameWorkTargets`) when the index is off or building. Capped at `MaxFranchiseMembers` (50), filtered through `VisibleTitlesAsync` before anything is written, and applied per member through `MutateAsync` so each row keeps its own event, revision and Undo.
- **The avoid channel is a scan-side buffer and never enters the pool.** A thumbs down, and a rating of 4 or under, put a title in `SeedSnapshot.Avoided`; `SemanticRecommender` scans those vectors as extra queries in the same row pass, in the text space, into their own `ScanBuffers.Avoid`. They must not be appended to `queries`: `FuseByRank`, the attribution walk and `MeasureQueries` all read that array whole, and a query whose job is to subtract would distort pool membership, the "feels like X" label and the per-query spread at once. Each candidate's best strength-weighted cosine above `RecommenderTuning.AvoidFloor` becomes an `avoidScore` in [0, 1] that `HybridScore` subtracts with `Weights.Avoid`. Only rows that reached the scored pool are computed, because the buffers are catalogue-sized. **`Weights.Avoid` ships at 12**, which is `rel-p12`: `AvoidNeutralize.Relative`, `AvoidBlend.Product`, `AvoidRelativeMargin` 1.0, `AvoidFloor` 0.45, `AvoidTagMinSupport` 2, `AvoidTagMargin` 1.0. The coefficient is large because the blend is zero on all but a handful of rows: a candidate pays only where a neutralized semantic score and a contrastive tag score are both positive. Numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` under "v5", "v5.1" and "v5.2: negative signals, neutralized". Do not move it without re-running `run-reco-suite.ps1`, which carries `noavoid` and `avoidnone12` beside the default, and `eval-reco.cs dial` beside that.
- **The semantic half is not a raw cosine either.** `RecommenderTuning.AvoidNeutralize` ships at `Relative`: it subtracts the row's own resemblance to the reader's seeds on the same floor and span, so a famous row near both sets cancels. `Relative` reads the raw best cosine over the text queries and never `bestCredit`, which under `QueryAttribution.Standardized` is a credit rather than a cosine. `PopResidual`, `Standardized` and `None` are measured controls kept so the harness can reproduce earlier rows; their numbers and failures are in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` under "v5.2".

- **What shipped is a near-clone filter, not a "less of this theme" dial, and the copy has to stay inside that.** `eval-reco.cs dial` is the gate that was NOT met: the bar was a named tag's share of the top 40 halving at N = 5, the best measured row is a 27% drop, and `rel-p12` itself moves two tags of four by 2.5 points and the other two by nothing at all. It was shipped on `pop` and nDCG with those numbers as the known ceiling. So the Lab and the menu say a dislike pushes down titles closer to what you rejected than to what you kept, which is the mechanism and is true; they must not promise a genre or a theme will recede.

- **The tag half of that penalty is a CONTRAST, not a subtraction of the avoided set.** `TagMath.BuildContrastiveProfile` keeps only what the avoided titles carry more of, per unit of share-normalized profile mass (`BuildProfile`), than the reader's own seeds do (`AvoidTagMargin`, 1.0), and only what at least `AvoidTagMinSupport` of them carry at class `Defining` or above. Without the contrast, a harem reader who disliked four harem titles would have every harem title marked down.
- **And the penalty is an AGREEMENT of the two halves, not a sum.** `RecommenderTuning.AvoidBlend` ships at `Product`, so either half at zero is no penalty: a candidate pays only when it sits near something the reader rejected AND carries the tags that made it a rejection. `Gate` (a threshold instead of a multiply), `Semantic` (v1) and `Tag` are measured controls. `TagMath.Score` is not bounded by 1 at the shipped `TagCandidateNormPower` of 0.75, so `BlendAvoid` clamps the tag side or the floor would mean a different thing at every norm power.
- **The `neg` column is reported and is NOT a gate**: a held-out dislike sits inside the reader's taste by construction, so gating on it would remove the neighbourhood the positives live in. nDCG and `pop` are the gates.
- **`eval-reco.cs dial` is a claim about the DIAL, never about quality**: it measures how far a named tag's share of the top 40, and every other tag with it, moves under injected dislikes. Quality is decided by `eval-reco-labels.cs --negatives` alone. N = 1 is a byte-identical no-op, which is `AvoidTagMinSupport` keeping the UI's one-thumbs-down promise.
- **A negative seed weight was the obvious design and is wrong.** `BuildQueries` builds a weighted centroid, so a negative weight moves it to a point on the sphere that stands for nothing, and every candidate is then scored against that point. "Less of this" is not "the opposite of this" - it is a second question, which is why it is a second channel.
- **`RecommendationFeedbackPolicy.AvoidRatingCeiling` is 4, and 5 stays neutral** because 5 maps to exactly 1.0 on the `rating / 5.0` scale an unrated title already gets. A low-rated row leaves the positive population **before** `Weigh` runs, not after: behavioural and personal-add weighting both fill in unrated rows, so a row still present would come back as a positive seed on read depth alone. `SeedSnapshot.Observed` keeps it - that half describes the shelf, and a profile chart that dropped everything a reader disliked would describe a different shelf. The avoided set is in `RecommendationService`'s pool cache key, or a dislike sits behind a twelve-hour hit. **This seed-side half has no isolated measurement** (the eval builds its own seed sets and never calls `SeedWeightService`), so do not quote the avoid-channel tables as evidence for it.
- **`SimilarSeriesService` is deliberately excluded from it.** Its cache is per seed rather than per user, and its heading is a claim about two works rather than about a reader. A per-user overlay is the follow-up if it turns out to matter; until then "More like this" answers the same question for everybody, which is also what its shared-weights test pins.
- **The explanation is display-only and must stay that way** (`TasteAvoidanceService`, `avoids` on `GET recommendations/feedback-lab`). It names a tag or genre over the avoided set at support >= 3, share >= 0.5 and at least `MinContrast` (0.3) above its share on the shelf, reusing `TasteGroupMining`'s specificity floor and clamp and `TasteInsightsService`'s `GroupCategories`/`DemographicGenres`. Nothing it produces reaches the ranking: "you avoid Harem" is a sentence about four books. The Lab copy says what the ranking does (a dislike pushes down titles closer to what you rejected than to what you kept), never "you avoid Harem".

- **The series page's "More like this" runs content channels only** (`SimilarSeriesService` passes `coGraph`, `coRead` and `taste` as `false`, so its cache key carries no channel flags). Its heading is a claim about two works, so a crowd-paired pick is off-topic there; turning a crowd channel back on means changing the heading too.

- **Two rail shapes, and which one a row gets is a data question, not a styling one** (`EngineRailRow` vs `DiscoverRailRow`, `frontend/src/components/ui/DiscoverRail.tsx`). The engine rail's card carries a per-item "why" line and the tags it matched on, which needs `coRead`/`coRecommended`/`tasteMatch`/`becauseOfTitle`/`matchedTags`, fields only recommender output sets. The catalogue rails and the reader-cohort rail hydrate straight from the dump and have none of them, so every card there would read "Similar feel": those stay on `DiscoverRailRow`. Any data-driven rail whose items carry those fields uses `EngineRailRow` (Home's "You might like", the series page's "More like this", Discover's recent-activity and side-interest rows, `recommendations` custom rails).
- **The reader-cohort surfaces are a hint, not a second score** (`Maki.Metadata.ReaderCohorts`, `{ConfigDir}/reader-cohorts.db`, kill switch `recommendations.readercohorts`). Groups of AniList readers with per-group completion and score aggregates; the instance places its own reader locally. A cohort mean is barely more accurate than the item mean and usually shows the same digits, so `ReaderCohortTuning.MinDivergence` gates the hint; numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` under "v5: reader cohorts".
- **The hint compares against the artifact's own all-readers mean, never against the MangaBaka rating rendered next to it**, which averages several providers, so gating on it would fire on a gap between two populations.
- **Cohort placement reads FINISHED series, never the shelf** — the same `TasteView.Read` against `TasteView.Shelf` distinction the taste page draws, sharing `BehavioralTasteService.ReadSignalsAsync` so the two cannot drift on incognito or root-folder visibility. Somebody with two hundred unread action titles and forty finished romcoms is a romcom reader.
- **The reader-cohort rail draws from each matched cohort in turn (`ReaderCohortService.Draw`); it never sums their scores into one list.** Lift is not comparable across cohorts, so `Σ w·lift` gives the strongest cohort every rank, and no rescaling fixes it. The draw costs recall@40 and raises cross-reader overlap on purpose (`distribution/CLAUDE.md`). `Place` weights are plain proportions for the same reason: floor-subtracting the weakest kept cohort distorts the mix, and under the draw that is screen space.
- **Verify any ranking change against the SIMULATED library, not the dev one.** `.devconfig` is nearly all BL, so one cohort taking every slot looks exactly like the rail working. `.simulated` places across five cohorts and is what surfaced the bug above.
- **The discovery artifacts unload when nothing reads them** (`ArtifactIdleUnloadJob`, `MAKI_ARTIFACT_IDLE_MINUTES`, default 30, 0 disables). `CatalogueIndexCache`, `CoReadCache`, `RecoGraphCache` and `ReaderCohortCache` each carry an `IdleStamp` and a `ReleaseIfIdle`. Size them by live heap, not the RSS delta while loading. **No lock, unlike the embedder's unload**: these hand out an immutable index and the caller holds its own reference, so clearing the field only makes it collectable once the last reader is done. The vector index unloads too, on its own longer window (`MAKI_VECTOR_IDLE_MINUTES`, default 60).
- **`ReaderCohortCache` is its own cache and must stay one.** Nothing it holds is scanned per catalogue row — placement is one lookup per series the reader finished — so it swaps a file in like the graph caches rather than invalidating the vector index the way `TasteVectorInstaller` has to. Its index is item-major CSR for that reason: the per-request question is "which cohorts carry this series", not "what does this cohort carry".
- **The taste page's groups are overlapping facet sets mined like frequent itemsets, not clusters** (`TasteGroupMining`, `TasteInsightsService`, `GET recommendations/taste-insights`): one tag or genre, plus pairs of them; count singles, keep what recurs, pair only those. A partition cannot say two habits share a book. `TasteClustering` kept only its vector math.
- **Cuts that keep a group's name specific.** Tags come from the index's packed blobs, never the dump's flat list, because the weight class and category only exist there: class >= `Defining` (`MinTagClass`) and category in `GroupCategories`. Genres carry no category, so `DemographicGenres` lists the demographic genres by hand. A genre whose name a tag already claims is dropped ("School Life" is both). `MinSingleSpecificity`: one broad facet is never a group, while two together can be ("Romance + Comedy").
- **`GroupCategories` (which `name_path` roots can name a group) is wider than `TagMath.IsStoryCategory` and narrower than the vocabulary**, adding `Species & Creatures` and `World Building`. Do not add `Locations` or `Narrative Tropes`: their head tags ("School", "High School", "Japan") are backdrops whose rarity is an artifact of tagging, so they clear any IDF floor, and `.simulated` named groups "Japan" and "High School + School Life".
- **Specificity is clamped at `MaxSpecificity`, an IDF of log(1/0.005)**, so past half a percent of the catalogue extra rarity buys no rank and a hyper-rare descriptive leaf cannot beat a real habit; below the ceiling, member count decides.
- **`MaxGroupsPerFacet` caps how many groups one facet may name, because `MaxOverlap` (a member-set test) cannot**: one facet can name several honestly different groups, and a reader skimming labels sees the same word repeated.
- **A pair scores as its rarer half plus a discounted share of the other** (`TasteGroupMining.PairSpecificity`: `max(a, b) + PairMinorWeight * min(a, b) * narrowing`, narrowing measured against the smaller facet's member count). A pair that narrows nothing ties its rarer half, and the tie-break on key count keeps the shorter name; scoring the halves as independent evidence would give every group a redundant second word.
- **One index scan per group, filtered to the group's own facets** - not just pointed at its centroid, which drifts a "Fake Relationship" rail into general romance within a few slots. Picks are the nearest unowned rows. Hydration is one dump read across every group, since the groups overlap and a read per group fetches the same rows a dozen times.
- **The per-group blind spot ("Nothing like these on your shelf") was removed** along with its `CosineBetween` scan (every hit × every owned series × every group), the only step quadratic in library size; restoring it restores that cost.
- **Home and Discover are user-arranged, through one merge** (`PageLayouts.Merge` over a `PageLayoutDefinition`; `ui.homesections` / `HomeLayoutSpec`, `ui.discoversections` / `DiscoverLayoutSpec`), which runs on every read and write; its rules are documented on `Merge`. Home's old `stats`/`progress`/`toread` keys migrate into one `glance` section via `HomeLayoutSpec.MigrateGlance`. On `PUT settings/ui` a null `DiscoverLayout` means leave the stored one alone, unlike `SeriesSections`, because every other caller of that endpoint would otherwise reset it. The editor (`components/layout/PageLayoutEditor`) edits a draft captured once on entering `?edit=1`: rail mutations invalidate the UI settings query, so re-syncing mid-edit would throw the draft away. Nothing on the page fetches while editing, and every Discover query is gated on the section that shows it.
- **Home ships opening on its hero, with discovery off.** `HomeSections.All` leads with `continue` then `jumpback` (the glance is not in it, see below); `recommended` and `popular` are in `HomeSections.DefaultOff` (via `PageLayoutDefinition.DefaultOff`, which only applies where a layout has never placed the key). `HomeLayoutSpec.MigrateHeroFirst` moves a stored layout that still equals the previous default (same order, everything on, no hero or panel flipped) onto the new one, so an untouched install follows; any layout the owner arranged is left alone. A reader who deliberately saves exactly the old default order is moved again on every read, which is the one arrangement that cannot be kept. Home has no title row: the hero is `items[0]` and the rest are the Next up queue, a tray of posters joined under the hero by default or, with the section's `nextUpBeside` switch, a list in a card beside it at the hero's height (`NextUpList`, `ContinueHero`'s `queue`). **The glance is not a section any more.** `HomeSections.Glance` is a retired key kept for migration: `HomeLayoutSpec.MigrateGlanceIntoContinue` folds a stored glance section into Continue reading's `glance` option (`PageSection.Options`, defaults in `HomeHeroOptions` and `HOME_SECTION_OPTIONS`, merged by `PageLayouts.MergeOptions` like hero flags). `HomePage` draws `ReadingWeeks` inside the Continue hero when that section leads with one and has something to continue, and as a strip at the section's slot otherwise; Jump back in never hosts it. Section options are drawn by `SectionProperties` from `SectionDef.options`, with `optionHint` for why one would show nothing (the glance needs read tracking and progression). `.continue-section` is the CSS container (`continue-lead`) and `ContinueHero` renders the glance twice, one copy always `display: none`: from `62rem` it is a card on the hero's right (`84rem` with Next up beside, since the hero is narrower then), on a phone (under `36rem`) the one-line form under the actions, and in between its own raised card under the lead (`.continue-glance-below`), never above the hero. `HomePage` holds the slot with `ReadingWeeksPlaceholder` while the summary and heatmap load, and draws nothing until the reader has read a day in the last five weeks. Home has a visually hidden h1; the hero title and Next up are h2. `frontend/src/api/hooks.ts` `HOME_SECTIONS` mirrors the server order.
- **Home's "recently added" rail reads `ChapterFile.DateAdded`**, not `StatsEvents` (which is aggregated to one row/series/day — can't name the newest chapter). Both reading rails do a bounded scan + in-memory group, not an unbounded `GROUP BY`, since after a Kavita import that'd aggregate the whole library on every page load.

- **Anime signals are a third seed source, below the shelf and below explicit feedback** (`AnimeSignal` entity, `AnimeSignalSyncService`, `AnimeSignalPolicy`, `GET/PUT/POST recommendations/anime-signals`, instance switch `recommendations.animesignals`, per-user opt-in `recommendations.animesignals.enabled` default off). The MangaBaka dump has no anime ids at all (`anime` is null on every row), so an anime is matched through the provider's own relation data: AniList `relations` on the list query. MAL's v2 API has no anime-to-manga relation field at all (`related_manga` always comes back empty), so a MAL row is resolved through AniList's public GraphQL instead, unauthenticated, by the anime's MAL id, one call per anime at 1s (AniList's unauthenticated ceiling is 90/min). Both are capped and throttled per pass and stamped `MatchAttemptedAtUtc` so re-syncs cost one list call. Novels are dropped before the dump lookup, otherwise a light-novel-sourced show resolves to nothing. The sync stores scored entries plus unscored Completed and Watching rows (the resume feature below needs those, and users often finish a show without scoring it); `AnimeSignalGrouping.Group` drops unscored rows before anything else, so an unscored row never seeds or avoids, and `AnimeSignalPolicy` and `AnimeSignalGroup.Score` take a non-null score for that reason (there is no unscored credit or unscored-drop arm to tune). In `SeedWeightService` an anime row is skipped when the manga is on the shelf, has a thumbs, or is an ignored source. Score >= 7 is a positive seed at `AnimeSignalPolicy.SeedWeightOf` (`score / 10.0 * SeedScaleOf(strength)`, see the strength bullet below); score <= 4 or Dropped (floored at `DroppedStrength`) goes into `Avoided` on the policy's own 10-point curve, not `RecommendationFeedbackPolicy.AvoidStrength`, which is a star scale. Positives sit in `Effective.EligibleIds` only, never `Observed` or `LibraryIds`, which is also what keeps the matched manga out of the output. `capabilities.animeSignals` on the feedback-lab payload means "instance on and an anime-capable tracker is connected", not "opted in": the client gates the opt-in switch on it.

- **Nothing reads raw `AnimeSignal` rows: everything goes through `AnimeSignalGrouping.Group`** (`Maki.Core/Recommendations/AnimeSignalGrouping.cs`, used by `SeedWeightService` and `AnimeSignalsController`). The stored rows say the same thing twice in two ways, and both of them inflate a signal. A reader scrobbling from Jellyfin to AniList *and* MyAnimeList has every show listed twice, and a franchise is one anime per season over one `MangaBakaId`. The order is fixed: **dedupe across trackers first on `MalAnimeId`** (AniList's `media.idMal`, trivially the row's own id on a "mal" row, backfilled for MAL rows by the `AnimeSignalMalAnimeId` migration and for AniList rows by the next sync), **then average the seasons' scores**. Averaging first would weight a show by how many trackers list it. Titles are never a dedupe fallback - AniList serves English and MAL romaji. Status is ranked Completed > Watching > OnHold > Dropped > Planning, so finishing any season outranks dropping another: somebody who watched three seasons and quit the fourth has not complained about the manga. Planning entries leave before the average so a score on an unwatched season cannot drag it, and unscored entries are skipped rather than counted as 0. `AnimeSignalPolicy` therefore takes `double` scores, not `int`: a group average is routinely fractional, and 6.5 has to read as neutral rather than round into a seed. The one other reader of raw rows is `AnimeSignalGrouping.Watched`, which runs the same tracker dedupe and then stops before the season merge, returning one `AnimeWatchedSeason` per anime; it exists for the resume feature and must never feed seeding.

- **"Start where the anime ended" joins the watch list to MangaBaka's `AnimeStart`/`AnimeEnd` and prefers undershooting the chapter number** (`AnimeCoverage.cs` is a C# port of `frontend/src/lib/animeCoverage.ts` and the two must stay in step, including the `reOnce` fallback that labels an unlabelled range `S1`; `AnimeResumeResolver.Resolve` is pure and documents its season, cour and film rules; `AnimeResumeService` adds library state; `AnimeResumeController`, `HomeController.FromAnime` (layout key `fromanime`) and `RecommendationController.Detail` compose it). The resolver returns null whenever ordering is unsafe, and every one of those nulls is deliberate: an overshoot ticks off chapters the reader never saw. Apply goes through `ReaderService.MarkWatchedAsync` and then `ImportSilentAsync` explicitly, because chapters 1..N are usually not downloaded and the mark recompute only sees files; skipping it makes the first real read emit one StatsEvent worth N+1 chapters. Dismissal stores the N it was dismissed at (`UserSeriesState.AnimeResumeDismissedAt`) so a finished second season brings the callout back. `AnimeCoverageBar` takes `hideResumeHint` so its own hint cannot contradict a partial-season callout.

- **Home's "Continue from the anime" rail also offers manga nobody has added** (`AnimeResumeService.CatalogueRailAsync`, after the library cards). Resolved off the dump's `anime_start`/`anime_end`/`total_chapters` (`MangaBakaLocalStore.GetAnimeCoverageAsync`) exactly as the Discover modal's catalogue callout is; "owned" is checked with `IgnoreQueryFilters`, because a copy in a root folder the reader cannot see still makes their add fail. Hidden titles, "not interested" feedback and the content ceiling (through `GetByIdsAsync`) keep a card off. Such a card carries its `MangaBakaRecommendation` and opens the Discover modal, whose Add has a "Mark ch. 1 to N watched" switch that calls apply on the new series. **A fresh add has no chapter rows yet** (source matching is deferred), so `ApplyAsync` raises the reading baseline as usual and, when nothing up to N exists to tick, stores N in `UserSeriesState.AnimeWatchPendingTo`. `ChapterSyncService` calls `AnimeResumePendingService` whenever a sync writes new chapters, which marks them watched in each pending reader's own `DataScope` and clears the mark. It applies once: a reader who later unmarks a chapter keeps it unmarked.

- **`AnimeSignalPrecedence` is the whole of "do not count one series twice"**: first-hand evidence (the manga on the shelf, a thumbs up or down, an ignored-as-seed override) replaces an anime signal rather than adding to it. `Reason` returns Library before Feedback before Ignored so the answer is stable. **`SeedWeightService` and `AnimeSignalsController` must both use the type rather than inlining the checks.** Superseded outranks the signal's own role in the payload, and `supersededBy` says which action did it. `counts` carries one number per role; never derive one by subtraction.

- **`AnimeSignalStrength` is the one number separating the three levels, and it scales the avoid channel as hard as the seed channel** (`AnimeSignalPolicy.RatingShareOf`/`SeedScaleOf`, per-user key `recommendations.animesignals.strength`, unset = `Balanced`). The level is a share of the rating the manga itself would carry (values documented on `RatingShareOf`); `Balanced` puts a loved anime at exactly 1.0, the neutral weight of an unrated shelf title. The avoid half matters most: people rate an adaptation for the adaptation, not the manga. `RoleOf` deliberately takes **no** level: the level scales how hard a signal pushes, never which way it points. No cache plumbing needed: seed weights and avoid values are already in `SeedSnapshot.Fingerprint` and the recommender's key.

- **Per-tracker anime opt-out lives under the anime-signals prefix, not `scrobble.{service}.*`** (`SettingKeys.RecommendationsAnimeSignalsSourceKey`, unset = on, `AnimeSignalSources.EnabledAsync`/`EnabledForAsync`, switch in `TrackerSyncControls` beside "Scrobble reading"/"Sync ratings"). Those three push manga *to* a tracker; this reads a different medium *from* one, and a reader can want AniList scrobbled with its anime list ignored. `ConnectionDto.AnimeList` is the capability (`tracker is IAnimeListSource`) and is what draws the switch; `PreferencesRequest.Anime` is nullable so the three blocks without the switch do not write an accidental off. Switching a source off deletes that service's rows immediately in `ScrobbleController.SetPreferences` and again defensively at the top of `AnimeSignalSyncService.RunAsync` - leaving them would keep a list the reader disabled in their taste for up to a day.
