# Recommendations, taste, home layout

- **EngineCard's `MatchedTags` must be safe to display.** Both semantic and fallback recommendation hydration remove tags marked `is_spoiler` in the candidate's own `tags_v2` before taking four labels. The embedding vocabulary's global spoiler flag is insufficient: a tag can be a spoiler for one series and ordinary for another. Keep spoiler filtering in hydration so it does not change ranking, and use the filtered list for card chips.

- **Discover's side-interest rows use minority core story tags from the visible shelf** (`SideInterestRailService`, `GET recommendations/discover/side-interests`). At least two distinct works must mark a tag core in the embedding index; only those works seed the row. Tags must be non-spoilers in `TagMath.IsStoryCategory`, excluding character traits even when marked core. All occurrences count toward the 35% library ceiling, so a common tag with few core assignments cannot become a minority interest. No genre or unweighted-tag fallback. Themes on works with fewer majority themes come first; overlapping supporting sets share one row. Fully incognito, below-neutral rated, and above-ceiling series are excluded before selecting themes. At most two cached recommendation pools produce up to 20 picks each, with a vector-index filter recheck because the SQL fallback cannot filter tags. These small rows have no expanded view; querying only their SeedIds would lose the theme constraint. No index or fewer than three matching picks means no row.

Migrated out of the root CLAUDE.md so this only loads when touching recommendation/discover/home-layout code.

- **The behavioural channel is a second vector space, not a third graph** (`Maki.Metadata.Taste`, `{ConfigDir}/taste-vectors.db`, kill switch `recommendations.tastevectors`). Item vectors factorized from AniList reading lists, scored as a cosine beside the text one in the same row pass. It reaches ~95k of the ~126k indexed rows against the co-read graph's ~41k, which is the point: a pair table is a lookup and is empty for most of the catalogue. `MangaBakaRecommendation.TasteMatch` is its own "why" and must not be folded into `CoRead` — they answer different questions off the same file, and switching co-read off still costs measurable relevance.
- **`VectorIndexCache` loads the taste artifact, so installing it invalidates the whole index** rather than swapping a file in the way the graph caches do. The scan needs a vector per row, and a dictionary lookup per candidate per query would cost more than the dot product it feeds. That is why `TasteVectorJob` runs last of the four staggered artifact downloads: doing it while the index is still building the first time throws that work away.
- **`Maki.Core.Recommendations.TasteTuning` and `Maki.Metadata.Taste.TasteVectorTuning` are unrelated.** The first is seed *weighting* (how much each of a reader's series steers the profile); the second is the behavioural *channel*. Any file using both needs one of them qualified, and `RecommendationService`'s local is `tasteVectors` because its constructor parameter `taste` is `BehavioralTasteService`.
- **Anything measured only against the crowd pair graphs is suspect in two specific directions** (details and numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md`): they over-represent famous titles, and they under-represent *affinity* — same creator, same franchise. A change that suppresses an affinity relationship looks free there and costs real relevance against held-out reading lists. Both biases surface in the eval's `pop` column before they surface anywhere else.
- **Discover's "Based on your recent activity" rail is the one per-user rail on that page** (`RecentActivityRailService`, `GET recommendations/discover/recent`), fetched separately from `GET discover` because those rails are cached once instance-wide with no viewer in scope. It only narrows the seeds — scoring, the owned-series exclusion, the rating clamp and the 12-hour pool cache all stay `RecommendationService`'s, which is also why it costs nothing on a warm pool. Seeds are the 6 most recently read series by `ReadCounts.ReadFor` (6 because `GetGroupedAsync` drew one card per seed in a three-column grid; the page renders the flat rail again, so the number is now only a seed count); `Incognito.Full` is excluded by hand here (its `ChapterProgress` rows exist, and the subtitle names the titles). Its `Feed` is not a `BrowseFeed` — the client branches on `DiscoverRail.SeedIds` to page the recommender instead of `GetFeedAsync`, which rejects the name.
- **Franchise crowding is fixed by re-ordering, never by dropping picks.** `RecommenderTuning.MaxPerFranchise` is the drop, it ships off, and the measurement on it says why: readers who finish something go on to read the rest of it, so suppressing franchise members costs nDCG. What people actually complain about is four spin-offs of one series filling the part of a rail they can see. So `RecommendationService` stamps each pick with its same-work component (`SemanticRecommender.FranchisesAsync`, the vector index's own `FranchiseGraph` column, so there is still one definition) and runs `Spread` over the similar pool: greedy, keeps everything, keeps each franchise's internal order, and only guarantees `FranchiseSpacing` (8, about one rail-width) between two members of one component. Relations are the other half and need a real cap, not spacing: `GetRelatedAsync` returns every seed's relations rated-best-first in one list, so `RecentActivityRailService.MaxRelatedPerFranchise` limits how many of the six leading slots any one franchise takes. Keyed on the component rather than on `RelatedToTitle` because two seeds can sit in the same franchise. The eval harness calls `SemanticRecommender` directly and never sees any of this, which is the point: the numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` still measure the ranker.
- **The grouped variant of that rail splits one pool, and cannot lean on `BecauseOfTitle` to do it** (`GetGroupedAsync`, `GET recommendations/discover/recent/grouped`). *Nothing in the frontend calls this route today: Discover went back to the flat rail because a grid of six cards, three 34px picks each, cost 520px and was harder to skim than one row of posters.* That field is null for a genre-only hit, and when the seeds share a genre that is nearly all of them: measured on `.simulated`, 200 similar picks over six seeds carried an attributed title on twelve, spread across two seeds, so grouping on it left four of six cards empty. `SeedFor` falls back to overlap against the pick's matched tags and genres, tags weighted 3:1 because a genre is shared by a third of the catalogue and says almost nothing about *which* seed a pick belongs to. Ties go to the seed holding fewer picks, which stops one strong seed taking the row.
- **The avoid channel is a scan-side buffer and never enters the pool.** A thumbs down, and a rating of 4 or under, put a title in `SeedSnapshot.Avoided`; `SemanticRecommender` scans those vectors as extra queries in the same row pass, in the text space, into their own `ScanBuffers.Avoid`. They must not be appended to `queries`: `FuseByRank`, the attribution walk and `MeasureQueries` all read that array whole, and a query whose job is to subtract would distort pool membership, the "feels like X" label and the per-query spread at once. Each candidate's best strength-weighted cosine above `RecommenderTuning.AvoidFloor` becomes an `avoidScore` in [0, 1] that `HybridScore` subtracts with `Weights.Avoid`. Only rows that reached the scored pool are computed, because the buffers are catalogue-sized. **`Weights.Avoid` ships at 0 and the whole channel is therefore inert**, measured across seven weight/floor configurations on 400 held-out reading lists: nothing drops the negative hit rate outside its interval without costing nDCG, and the `pop` column says the one significant configuration is removing famous titles rather than similar ones. Numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` under "v5: negative signals" and "v5.1: negative signals, the blend". Do not turn it on without re-running `run-reco-suite.ps1`, which now carries `noavoid`, `avoidrp12`, `avoidps3` and `--negatives`.
- **The semantic half is not a raw cosine either, and that is the one change that worked.** `RecommenderTuning.AvoidNeutralize` decides what it measures: `Relative` (the default) subtracts the row's own resemblance to the reader's seeds on the same floor and span, so a famous row near both sets cancels; `PopResidual` subtracts the mean raw score of the row's own popularity bucket over the scored pool; `Standardized` is the per-query control and fails on `pop` the way v1 did; `None` is v1 and v2, kept so the harness can reproduce those rows. Five configurations now clear both ranking gates, which neither v1 nor v2 ever did. The channel still ships at 0 because `eval-reco.cs dial` says so: nothing halves a named tag's share of the page, and any `Semantic` blend also breaks the N = 1 no-op, since `AvoidTagMinSupport` lives on the tag half. Numbers in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` under "v5.2: negative signals, neutralized". `Relative` reads the raw best cosine over the text queries and never `bestCredit`, which under `QueryAttribution.Standardized` is a credit rather than a cosine.

- **The tag half of that penalty is a CONTRAST, not a subtraction of the avoided set.** `TagMath.BuildContrastiveProfile` keeps only what the avoided titles carry more of, per unit of profile mass, than the reader's own seeds do (`AvoidTagMargin`, 1.0), and only what at least `AvoidTagMinSupport` of them carry at class `Defining` or above. Both profiles are share-normalized by `BuildProfile`, which is the only reason five dislikes and ninety shelf titles can be subtracted at all. Without the contrast the honest reading of a 40-tag famous dislike is "this reader avoids their own genre": a harem reader who disliked four harem titles would have every harem title on their page marked down, which is the failure the semantic half does not have.
- **And the penalty is an AGREEMENT of the two halves, not a sum.** `RecommenderTuning.AvoidBlend` ships at `Product`, so either half at zero is no penalty: a candidate pays only when it sits near something the reader rejected AND carries the tags that made it a rejection. `Gate` is the same idea with a threshold instead of a multiply, `Semantic` is v1 and `Tag` is the control; all four are swept and none of them ships, because `pop` moved outside its band in every configuration. `TagMath.Score` is not bounded by 1 at the shipped `TagCandidateNormPower` of 0.75, so `BlendAvoid` clamps the tag side or the floor would mean a different thing at every norm power.
- **The `neg` column is reported and is NOT a gate.** A held-out dislike is a title the reader chose to read and then rated, which puts it inside their taste by construction, so a channel that removed those would be removing the neighbourhood the positives live in. nDCG and `pop` are the gates; `neg` is there to show the channel is doing something at all.
- **`eval-reco.cs dial` is a claim about the DIAL, never about quality.** It injects the N most popular catalogue titles carrying a named tag as dislikes (popular on purpose: real dislikes skew famous) and reports what is left of that tag in the top 40, how much every other tag moved with it, `pop`, and Jaccard overlap. Quality is decided by `eval-reco-labels.cs --negatives` and nowhere else. Its one clean result is that N = 1 is a byte-identical no-op, which is `AvoidTagMinSupport` keeping the UI's promise by arithmetic rather than by wording.
- **A negative seed weight was the obvious design and is wrong.** `BuildQueries` builds a weighted centroid, so a negative weight moves it to a point on the sphere that stands for nothing, and every candidate is then scored against that point. "Less of this" is not "the opposite of this" - it is a second question, which is why it is a second channel.
- **`RecommendationFeedbackPolicy.AvoidRatingCeiling` is 4, and 5 stays neutral** because 5 maps to exactly 1.0 on the `rating / 5.0` scale an unrated title already gets. A low-rated row leaves the positive population **before** `Weigh` runs, not after: behavioural and personal-add weighting both fill in unrated rows, so a row still present would come back as a positive seed on read depth alone. `SeedSnapshot.Observed` keeps it - that half describes the shelf, and a profile chart that dropped everything a reader disliked would describe a different shelf. The avoided set is in `RecommendationService`'s pool cache key, or a dislike sits behind a twelve-hour hit. **This seed-side half is what actually ships**, since the channel itself is off: a 1/10 used to pull at weight 0.2 and now pulls at nothing. It has an obvious argument and no isolated measurement - the eval builds its own seed sets and never calls `SeedWeightService` - so do not quote the avoid-channel tables as evidence for it.
- **`SimilarSeriesService` is deliberately excluded from it.** Its cache is per seed rather than per user, and its heading is a claim about two works rather than about a reader. A per-user overlay is the follow-up if it turns out to matter; until then "More like this" answers the same question for everybody, which is also what its shared-weights test pins.
- **The explanation is display-only and must stay that way** (`TasteAvoidanceService`, `avoids` on `GET recommendations/feedback-lab`). It counts tags and genres over the avoided set and names one at support >= 3 and share >= 0.5, reusing `TasteGroupMining`'s specificity floor and clamp and `TasteInsightsService`'s `GroupCategories`/`DemographicGenres` rather than copying them - without the floor the honest answer for almost any set of titles is "School" or "Japan". Nothing it produces reaches the ranking: "you avoid Harem" is a sentence about four books, and acting on it would demote several thousand. **The UI copy has to stay inside what ships** - with `Weights.Avoid` at 0 nothing pushes similar titles down, so the Lab says a dislike stops a title steering recommendations, which is the part that is true.

- **The series page's "More like this" runs content channels only** (`SimilarSeriesService`). `coGraph`, `coRead` and `taste` are passed `false` unconditionally, not read from the instance settings the way Discover reads them, and the cache key carries no channel flags because of it. The rail's heading is a claim about the two works, so a pick whose only link to the seed is that a crowd paired the titles is off-topic there however good a recommendation it is - and it announced itself, since `engineWhy` prints "Readers like you also finished this" on any pick carrying `coRead`. What is left is feel, genres, tags and author. Turning a crowd channel back on here means changing the heading too.

- **Two rail shapes, and which one a row gets is a data question, not a styling one** (`EngineRailRow` vs `DiscoverRailRow`, `frontend/src/components/ui/DiscoverRail.tsx`). The engine rail's card carries a per-item "why" line and the tags it matched on, which needs `coRead`/`coRecommended`/`tasteMatch`/`becauseOfTitle`/`matchedTags` — fields only recommender output sets. The catalogue rails and the reader-cohort rail hydrate straight from the dump and have none of them, so every card there would read "Similar feel": those stay on `DiscoverRailRow`. Today the engine rail is Discover's recent-activity row, the series page's "More like this" and Home's "You might like".
- **The reader-cohort surfaces are a hint, not a second score, and that was a measurement rather than a taste call** (`Maki.Metadata.ReaderCohorts`, `{ConfigDir}/reader-cohorts.db`, kill switch `recommendations.readercohorts`). Groups of AniList readers with their per-group completion and score aggregates; the instance places its own reader locally. A cohort mean beats the plain item mean on held-out readers by 0.12 points on a 100-point scale — real, and 0.012 of a rendered star. Its median distance from that mean is 1.41 points, so shown on every series it is the same digits as the aggregate beside it nine times in ten. `ReaderCohortTuning.MinDivergence` is what turns it into something worth showing; the numbers are in `distribution/CLAUDE.md` under "v5: reader cohorts".
- **The hint compares against the artifact's own all-readers mean, never against the MangaBaka rating rendered next to it.** That aggregate averages several metadata providers; the cohorts are one crowd on one site. On DICE the catalogue says 7.0 and the reader crowd says 6.6, so gating on the visible number would fire the hint on a gap between two populations rather than on anything about the reader.
- **Cohort placement reads FINISHED series, never the shelf** — the same `TasteView.Read` against `TasteView.Shelf` distinction the taste page draws, sharing `BehavioralTasteService.ReadSignalsAsync` so the two cannot drift on incognito or root-folder visibility. Somebody with two hundred unread action titles and forty finished romcoms is a romcom reader.
- **The reader-cohort rail draws from each matched cohort in turn; it never sums their scores into one list.** Lift is not comparable across cohorts — a tight cohort produces far higher completion rates than a broad one — so `Σ w·lift` gives the strongest cohort every rank, and a reader with 15 finished romances and 3 action manhwa got a rail of nothing but action manhwa. No rescaling fixes it (max-normalisation and reciprocal rank fusion were both measured returning the identical list); the draw in `ReaderCohortService.Draw` is the fix. It costs recall@40 on the average reader and roughly triples cross-reader overlap, which is a deliberate trade written up in `distribution/CLAUDE.md`. `Place` weights are plain proportions for the same reason: floor-subtracting the weakest kept cohort turned a 1.8:1 affinity into a 2.5:1 mix, and under the draw that is screen space.
- **Verify any ranking change against the SIMULATED library, not the dev one.** `.devconfig` is nearly all BL, so one cohort taking every slot looks exactly like the rail working. `.simulated` places across five cohorts and is what surfaced the bug above.
- **The discovery artifacts unload when nothing reads them** (`ArtifactIdleUnloadJob`, `MAKI_ARTIFACT_IDLE_MINUTES`, default 30, 0 disables). `CatalogueIndexCache`, `CoReadCache`, `RecoGraphCache` and `ReaderCohortCache` each carry an `IdleStamp` and a `ReleaseIfIdle`; together they are ~72 MB of live heap on a 126k-row catalogue, which an instance nobody is browsing has no use for. Quote the LIVE figure and not the RSS delta each one shows as it loads - the catalogue indexes read 52 MB loading and 33.5 MB live, because the difference is heap the build churned through and the unload cannot give that back. **No lock, unlike the embedder's unload**: these hand out an immutable index and the caller holds its own reference, so clearing the field only makes it collectable once the last reader is done. **The vector index is in the set too, on its own longer window** (`MAKI_VECTOR_IDLE_MINUTES`, default 60). It was originally excluded - every recommendation and every search needs it, so it is the likeliest to be wanted again immediately and the most expensive to rebuild - and a measurement reversed that: a real instance at 16 idle minutes held 104 MB of large-object heap with the vectors the largest single part of it and nobody searching. The original reasoning now lives in the window rather than in an exemption, so an instance that gets searched twice an hour never meets it.
- **`ReaderCohortCache` is its own cache and must stay one.** Nothing it holds is scanned per catalogue row — placement is one lookup per series the reader finished — so it swaps a file in like the graph caches rather than invalidating the vector index the way `TasteVectorInstaller` has to. Its index is item-major CSR for that reason: the per-request question is "which cohorts carry this series", not "what does this cohort carry".
- **The taste page's groups are mined as overlapping facet sets, not clustered** (`TasteGroupMining`, `TasteInsightsService`, `GET recommendations/taste-insights`). This replaced spherical k-means over the embedding vectors, and the reason is expressiveness rather than clustering quality: a partition has to account for every series once, so at the two to five groups a library supports each one is half a shelf and its label is whatever separates one half from the other ("Japan + Primarily Teen Cast"). What a reader actually has is a dozen habits that share members, and a partition cannot say that about the same book twice. The unit is now one tag or genre, plus pairs of them, mined like frequent itemsets: count singles, keep what recurs, pair only those. `TasteClustering` kept only its vector math; the k-means is gone.
- **Three cuts are what keep a group's name specific, and each of them was put there by a label that shipped wrong.** Tags come from the index's packed blobs, never the dump's flat list, because the weight class and the category only exist there: class >= `Defining`, and `TagMath.IsStoryCategory` only, which is what stops "Primarily Teen Cast" and "Full Colour" naming a group. Genres carry no category at all, so `DemographicGenres` lists the same five names by hand - without it the simulated shelf's top group was "Shounen" over 53% of it. A genre whose name a tag already claimed is dropped ("School Life" is both, and keying them separately mined a pair reading "School Life + School Life"). `MinSingleSpecificity` is the fourth: one broad facet is never a group, while two of them together can be, which is the only way "Romance + Comedy" can exist.
- **Which `name_path` roots can name a group is its own set (`GroupCategories`), wider than `TagMath.IsStoryCategory` and narrower than the vocabulary.** The story set covers 2471 of 6427 tags and is tuned for a different job - constraining a scan in `SideInterestRailService`, where a creature tag drags in noise - so borrowing it dropped "Monsters" and "Magic" and a progression-fantasy library could not form "Monsters + Skills" at all. `Species & Creatures` and `World Building` are added. `Locations` and `Narrative Tropes` were tried and taken back out, and that is the part worth remembering: Locations holds "Dungeon" (df 820) and every reason to want it, but its head is "School" (40037), "High School" (6130) and "Japan" (5154) - backdrops whose catalogue rarity is an artifact of nobody tagging them rather than of the trait being rare, so they clear any specificity floor an IDF can express. With that root in, `.simulated` named "Japan" and "High School + School Life", which is the exact label this surface exists to stop printing.
- **Specificity is clamped at `MaxSpecificity`, an IDF of log(1/0.005).** IDF is log(1/share) whatever the corpus size, so the clamp is corpus-independent: past half a percent of the catalogue, extra rarity buys no rank. Unclamped, a hyper-rare descriptive leaf beats a real habit on almost no evidence - "Comedic Facial Expressions" (`Themes > Comedy > …`, 164 series, four of the simulated shelf) outscored groups four times its size, and a category cut cannot remove it because the taxonomy files it correctly. Below the ceiling, member count decides between rare facets, which is the evidence question the raw IDF was answering backwards.
- **`MaxGroupsPerFacet` caps how many groups one facet may name, and `MaxOverlap` cannot do its job.** Overlap is a test on member sets: "Student-Student Relationship" named four of twelve groups on `.simulated`, once alone and three times in a pair, and every member set was different enough to pass honestly. A reader skims labels, so one word four times is what they see whoever the members are. `SelectionPool` is 800 rather than 400 because a library built on few facets now rejects most of its own ranking before filling the page.
- **A pair scores as its rarer half plus however much the other one narrowed it** (`TasteGroupMining.PairSpecificity`). Every fake-relationship story a reader owns is also a romance, so "Romance + Fake Relationship" covers the same books as "Fake Relationship" with a longer label. Scoring the two names as independent evidence makes a redundant pair beat every single it is made of, and every group on the page grows a second word. Narrowing measured against the smaller of the two facets' own member counts leaves a pair that narrows nothing tied with its rarer half, and the selection's tie-break on key count then keeps the shorter name.
- **One index scan per group, filtered to the group's own facets** - not just pointed at its centroid, which drifts a "Fake Relationship" rail into general romance within a few slots. Picks are the nearest unowned rows. Hydration is one dump read across every group, since the groups overlap and a read per group fetches the same rows a dozen times.
- **The per-group blind spot ("Nothing like these on your shelf") was built and then removed, and the reason is the surface, not the idea.** It was worth a card when there were two to five broad clusters; at twelve narrow groups it is twelve of them, and a reader skimming their own shelf reads that as noise. Removing it also took out the build's most expensive step: `CosineBetween` over every scan hit × every owned series × every group, which is quadratic in library size where nothing else here is. `TasteBlindSpot`, `BlindSpotFrom` and the near-owned filter are gone rather than left computed and unrendered; restoring them means restoring that cost too.
- **Home sections are user-ordered** (`ui.homesections`, `HomeLayoutSpec`). `Merge` runs on every read/write: unknown keys dropped, new keys appended enabled (never re-slotted) so a release adding a section doesn't scramble the user's ordering.
- **Home's "recently added" rail reads `ChapterFile.DateAdded`**, not `StatsEvents` (which is aggregated to one row/series/day — can't name the newest chapter). Both reading rails do a bounded scan + in-memory group, not an unbounded `GROUP BY`, since after a Kavita import that'd aggregate the whole library on every page load.
